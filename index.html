🌌 The Mission

Close-approach events between satellites are rising sharply as constellations scale into the thousands[cite: 1]. Operators today rely on manual conjunction review or simple decentralized avoidance rules, which are slow, propellant-wasteful, and increasingly untenable as encounter rates grow[cite: 1]. On the other hand, fully learned (black-box) collision-avoidance policies are hard to certify or explain to regulators and insurers[cite: 1].

OrbitFlow is a hierarchical planning system that pairs an interpretable, physics-grounded planner with a coordination layer that resolves multi-satellite conflicts efficiently[cite: 1].

⚙️ System Architecture

The Generator: A* Search

Generates a discrete set of safe, low-cost trajectory candidates per satellite[cite: 1]. Orbital dynamics and collision risk are encoded directly into the search, ensuring a structured and auditable process—not a black box[cite: 1].

The Coordinator: MARL Policy

A multi-agent coordination layer selects joint maneuvers across satellites to minimize total propellant while enforcing separation constraints[cite: 1]. This avoids the inefficient equilibria that arise when satellites avoid each other independently[cite: 1].

The Framework: ECI/LVLH

A hybrid architecture keeps the system physically realistic[cite: 1]. Full orbit propagation and conjunction screening occur in ECI, while local relative-motion planning happens in LVLH to keep the problem tractable[cite: 1].

The Guarantee: Safety Filter

Every maneuver proposed by the MARL layer is checked against a constraint-enforcement safety filter before execution[cite: 1]. Separation guarantees hold even if the learned policy behaves unexpectedly, providing the certifiability story insurers need[cite: 1].

🤝 Competitive Advantage

Most space traffic management (STM) companies today—such as Kayhan Space, Slingshot Aerospace, COMSPOC, ExoAnalytic Solutions, and Neuraspace—sit at the detection and decision-support layer[cite: 1]. They ingest tracking data and hand each operator a single-satellite maneuver recommendation[cite: 1]. Even the newest automation treats each satellite's avoidance decision independently[cite: 1].

  • Intelligence Layer: We generate the candidate maneuver space itself, grounded in orbital dynamics and risk[cite: 1].
  • Joint Management: Our multi-agent layer optimizes maneuvers jointly across all satellites in an encounter—the coordination problem most STM providers don't touch[cite: 1].
